Not every idea needs to become a company. Sometimes the most useful software is the little thing that helps you find what you need, remember a task, or make sense of a spreadsheet.

Personal software starts with a person and a real need. It can be useful to an audience of one, and it can change as you learn what actually helps.

Begin with a moment of friction

Think of a task you repeat: collecting notes before a meeting, finding a document you saved last month, or keeping track of questions you want to ask. What part takes more effort than it should?

Choose one small improvement. You don’t need a grand plan or a polished app. A place to keep the information, a way to find it again, and a simple action might be enough for a first version.

Give the idea a shape

Imagine keeping track of useful things you’ve read. Start with a title, a link, and one sentence about why you saved it. The first useful action might simply be finding an item by title. Later, you could add a topic or a reminder to revisit it.

Write down two or three example entries and the result you’d like to see. This gives you something concrete to work towards and something to check. If a spreadsheet already solves the problem well, you don’t have to replace it; the goal is to make the task easier, not add another task.

Let your tools help you build

mAIcro gives you a place to work with data, documents, and processes. It includes an IDE and a GraphQL API; making a tool may involve learning some technical steps or working with an AI assistant. You don’t have to understand everything at once.

You could ask an assistant to help you plan the information to keep, explain an unfamiliar step, or suggest how to test the result. It doesn’t need to make every decision for you. Start with information you’re comfortable experimenting with, and check the results before relying on them.

As you try your idea, notice what works and what doesn’t. Can you add an entry and find it again? What happens when a link is missing? Change one thing, try again, and keep the parts that make your day easier.

Make it yours

Running mAIcro locally gives you a space to experiment on your own computer. If you connect an external AI service or API, information you send to it may leave your machine, so review what you share.

There is no requirement to turn your idea into a product for everyone. Sometimes making something useful for yourself is more than enough.
